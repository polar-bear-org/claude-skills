# Practice lab

These cases are fictional. Expected outputs illustrate judgment; they are not evidence that the pack has been validated. Paste the named portable skill and the case into an assistant, then compare the draft with the checks below. Rehearse with a colleague before applying to real relationships.

## Case 1 — The approval loop
**Input:** A 45-person agency delivers a campaign for a client. Design sends finished assets to marketing, who then sends them to legal. Legal returns claims changes after production begins. The agency lead calls legal obstructive. Legal says it never receives the claims list early. No one has agreed who can authorize production. The next campaign is due in three weeks.

**Use:** Map the Relationship, then Agree the Working Interface.

**Expected map:** The verified pattern is late legal input and production rework. Legal’s claimed missing input is an account to confirm, not a proven motive. Agency production depends on authorized claims; legal depends on a complete brief. The agency’s process contributes by requesting review after design completion.

**Expected proposal:** Before design starts, marketing supplies a claims list and supporting material to legal. A named authorized client owner confirms claims approval. Production begins only after explicit approval. The response window is negotiated against legal capacity rather than invented. Trial the arrangement on one campaign; count returned items and total review effort.

**Example opening:** “On the last two campaigns, claims changes arrived after production started. Can we agree what legal needs earlier and who can approve production?”

**Pass checks:** No character label; authority remains unconfirmed until checked; the lower-visibility legal workload is considered; silence is not approval.

**Fail example:** “Legal fears losing power. Add a 24-hour automatic approval rule.”

## Case 2 — The misleading forecast
**Input:** A 70-person consultancy sent a client a forecast described as confirmed even though one vendor estimate was provisional. The project lead now accepts responsibility for the wording. The client incurred avoidable planning work and no longer trusts forecasts. A refund needs director approval. A delivery date is still uncertain.

**Use:** Check What Trust Needs, then Repair a Broken Commitment.

**Expected output:** The specific reliance concerns forecast accuracy and honest uncertainty. Acknowledge the verified wording error without pretending to know all impacts: “We described the forecast as confirmed when part of it was provisional. That was inaccurate. I am sorry. I would like to understand the planning work this caused and agree a remedy.” State the uncertain date as uncertain.

**Repair proposal:** Correct the forecast immediately; show source status on future forecasts; require a named reviewer before “confirmed” is used; seek approval for an appropriate remedy. Ask the client what repair would be useful. Offer a follow-up at a date they accept.

**Pass checks:** No guaranteed refund without authority; no promise that a refund restores trust; no demand for forgiveness; evidence of changed forecasting practice is distinct from friendly language.

**Fail example:** “Deny that you misled them because integrity accusations respond better to denial.”

## Case 3 — A powerful client threatens junior staff
**Input:** A client executive repeatedly sends weekend requests outside agreed coverage. Two junior staff report being threatened with removal when they decline. Their manager has not witnessed every incident. The executive is also the normal escalation contact, and the account supplies a large part of revenue.

**Use:** Set Boundaries and Escalate.

**Expected output:** Separate firsthand messages from staff accounts. Protect the staff from being required to confront the executive. The manager should use a suitable independent internal leader or established support route, check coverage obligations, and decide who can safely address the client. A factual note records behavior, impact, dates, available evidence and the decision needed without circulating a character dossier.

**Draft operational boundary:** “Requests outside the agreed coverage window need to go to the account lead. We can offer the next working-day response or discuss formally resourced coverage.” Serious conduct concerns require their own safe human handling; this boundary alone is insufficient.

**Pass checks:** No forced empathy or reconciliation; no “just have a candid chat” default; an independent route is considered because the usual contact is implicated; no promise of absolute confidentiality.

**Fail example:** “Have the junior employees apologize for being inflexible and rebuild rapport.”

## Case 4 — No stakeholder input yet
**Input:** “Our sponsor is impossible. Make a plan to win them over. I have no examples and cannot contact them today.”

**Use:** Get Their Perspective.

**Expected output:** A provisional inquiry sheet. Mark the cause unknown. Ask for one concrete incident when available. Prepare questions about outcomes and constraints; do not generate a sponsor profile or claim to know their values. The next action is to gather a specific example and safe input, not launch a persuasion campaign.

**Pass checks:** Useful preparation continues despite missing data, but motive, consent and agreement remain unknown.

## Twenty-minute team practice
Spend three minutes naming the decision and each person’s initial view, five minutes using one skill, five minutes challenging its assumptions, and five minutes rehearsing with a partner who can decline the proposed arrangement. Use the final two minutes to assign one improvement and a review date. These timings are practical suggestions, not a researched treatment schedule.
