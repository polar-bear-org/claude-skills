# Ways of Working Change Pack

**10 practical AI skills for changing how work gets done, from a concrete behavior to a tested rollout.** For founders and managers in agencies and professional-services teams of roughly 20–200.

Use this for a new intake process, delivery workflow, approval step or tool-enabled practice. You leave with usable change briefs, barrier checks, pilot plans and decisions supported by evidence. Routine team agreements and retrospectives belong in the Team Performance Pack; this pack handles introducing, testing and sustaining a different way of working.

## Start in five minutes
1. Open `portable/change-behavior-brief.md`, paste the complete prompt into your assistant and describe a real change with your initial thinking.
2. Review the behavior brief with someone who does the work.
3. Choose the next skill for the uncertainty you actually face. There is no compulsory sequence.

## Use with your assistant
For a single conversation, paste an individual file from `portable/`. For a project, add `portable/ALL-SKILLS.md` as a source and use `PROJECT-INSTRUCTIONS.md` as project instructions. If retrieval misses the selected instructions, paste its individual prompt. These are text prompts; assistant features and context limits vary.

For an assistant supporting Agent Skills, use selected folders from `skills/` according to its instructions. The plugin manifest is included for packaging. Marketplace registration and public download availability follow publication; this draft does not claim an installed integration.

## Human judgment
People determine what work should change, hear concerns and make decisions. AI helps organize and challenge the plan. Use minimal, sanitized information. Outputs are drafts; no skill sends messages, monitors employees or changes systems. Mandatory or formal employment changes need the responsible human process.

Research supports parts of the design, with much intervention evidence coming from healthcare. Transfer to agency work is a reasoned application to test locally. This pack is an original practice resource, not a validated intervention or a promise of successful change.
