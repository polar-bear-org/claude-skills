# House style

The rules all three skills read before touching a word. Edit this file to change how
the pack writes; the skills carry the method, this file carries your taste.

## The defaults

**Say the thing.** The point goes in the first sentence of the paragraph, not the last.
Readers stop early, and a conclusion nobody reached is a conclusion nobody has.

**Concrete over abstract.** "Three of the eight managers missed the deadline" beats
"there were some completion challenges". Where a number exists, use it; where it does
not, say so rather than reaching for a vague quantifier.

**No corporate throat-clearing.** Banned openings: "In today's fast-paced world",
"It is important to note that", "As we all know", "This document aims to". They
signal that the writing has not started yet.

**Hedge once, or not at all.** "This might possibly suggest that we could perhaps
consider" is four hedges doing the work of none. Pick the one that is honest and drop
the rest.

**Short words win.** Use, not utilise. Start, not commence. Help, not facilitate.
The exception is when the long word is precise and the short one is not.

## Rhythm

Vary sentence length deliberately. A run of same-length sentences reads as machine
output regardless of the words in them. Follow a long, qualified sentence with a short
one. Like this.

Paragraphs of one sentence are allowed and often the strongest thing on the page.
Paragraphs over six lines are usually two paragraphs.

## Formatting

- **Bold** carries the scannable claim, not random emphasis. One bold phrase per
  paragraph at most.
- Bullets are for genuine lists — parallel items, no argument between them. An
  argument in bullet form is an argument with its connective tissue removed.
- Headings are promises. A heading the section does not deliver is worse than none.
- Tables beat prose whenever the reader is comparing things.

## What never gets touched

Quotations, names, figures, and anything inside code fences. If a quote is awkward, it
stays awkward — that is what quoting means. Flag it instead of fixing it.

## The final check

Read the first sentence and the last sentence together. If a reader who saw only those
two would get the point, the piece is structured. If not, the problem is not the words.
