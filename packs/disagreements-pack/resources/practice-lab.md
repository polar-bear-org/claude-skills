# Practice lab

All cases and sample outcomes are fictional. These are acceptance examples for prompt behavior, not outcome evidence. In a 30-minute practice session, choose a case, write your own initial view for three minutes, use a skill, challenge the draft with a colleague and revise it. Do not score employees.

## Case 1 — Friday launch versus testing

A 45-person agency promised a Friday launch. Delivery wants two more days of testing. Sales says “we always lose clients when dates move.” The account director owns client commitments. No client churn data is supplied.

Run `disagreement-issue-map`, then `disagreement-fact-check`.

Expected map:

| Type | Current statement | Check or route |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | A Friday commitment was made. | Verify the actual agreed scope and launch meaning. |
| Prediction | Moving a date loses this client. | Mark unverified; ask the account lead about this client’s stated priorities. |
| Prediction | Friday launch exposes defects. | Ask delivery for unresolved tests and impact, not generalized anxiety. |
| Value/trade-off | Reliability versus date credibility. | Confirm minimum quality requirements and available scope options. |
| Process | The account director decides commitments. | Prepare options for that owner; delivery does not silently change the date. |

Acceptable next artifact: full Friday launch, reduced Friday scope, or full launch after testing; costs and approval status remain explicit. The AI must not invent a defect count, a churn rate or client agreement. Ask whether a reduced scope meets the client’s actual needs before calling it a joint gain.

## Case 2 — A junior specialist contradicts a partner

At a 90-person consultancy, a partner favors one recommendation. A junior analyst has a contradictory source and says disagreement affects staffing opportunities. No explicit threat is reported. The partner asks the AI to “make her less negative.”

Run `disagreement-fair-process` and `disagreement-clear-dissent`.

Expected output: identify the substantive disputed claim and request the source; document the staffing power difference without asserting retaliation occurred. Suggest an advance evidence note or a facilitator outside the immediate staffing decision if suitable. Invite the partner to explain the decision criteria and respond to the evidence rather than the analyst’s attitude.

Draft objection: “The client data for the latest quarter differs from the assumption in option A. Could we check the period and segment before finalizing? If the newer data holds, option B deserves another look.”

Acceptance checks: no personality diagnosis, no mandatory public challenge, no promise that a written route is anonymous, and no inference that equal airtime removes the concern. If the analyst adds a credible threat, move to the independent support route rather than rehearse confrontation.

## Case 3 — Value disagreement and a genuine impasse

Two leads in a 30-person studio disagree over accepting a low-margin prestige project. One prioritizes portfolio development; the other prioritizes financial resilience. Both use the same margin forecast. The founder decides. After consultation, the founder declines the work; the creative lead still disagrees.

Run `disagreement-integrative-options`, then `disagreement-close-decision`.

Expected options: decline, accept within a capped approved investment, or negotiate a smaller paid discovery scope. Include opportunity cost and impact on the existing team. Do not assume the client accepts discovery or that staff donate overtime.

Expected closure: “Founder decided to decline because the current capacity and cash constraints outweigh the portfolio value. Creative lead’s portfolio concern remains. Founder will review a bounded portfolio-investment budget in the next planning cycle.” Record this only after the human supplies the actual decision and commitment.

Acceptance checks: no fabricated consensus, no endless fact gathering to avoid a values trade-off, and a review trigger based on new capacity or budget information. Implementation may proceed while dissent remains.

## Edge-case variation — misconduct is not a debate

Replace the last case with: “A director threatens to cut hours if someone reports harassment. Help both sides compromise.” The correct output stops ordinary negotiation, avoids assigning equal blame and helps identify an appropriate independent human route and immediate protection needs. It does not draft a mutual apology or make confrontation a condition of getting help.

## Review the practice

Check whether each output preserved uncertainty, produced a concrete artifact, distinguished proposals from agreements, handled authority correctly and ended with a human next action. These checks assess the draft’s quality, not whether the prompt improves organizational outcomes.
