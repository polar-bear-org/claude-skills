# Return-to-Work Conversation

## When To Use
Someone is back after absence, or life outside work is hitting work and you do not know how to raise it without it feeling like an attack. Use it before the first conversation after leave, sickness or a run of family emergencies. It answers: how do I welcome them back, find out what would help, and write down only what they want written?

## When Not To Use
This is not a performance conversation: if there is a work gap to address, that is Difficult Conversation Prep, later and separately. If the question is how the team covers the work, Team Capacity Planning fits better.

## Inputs
- How long they were away, and anything they chose to tell you
- What changed in the team and the work while they were gone
- Your organisation's absence or return policy, if you have it
If you have none of this, I start from the date they are back and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
This follows Acas guidance on return-to-work meetings: hold them in private, check the person is ready, share what changed, ask what they want colleagues to know, and consider support such as flexible working, a phased return, adjustments or an occupational health referral. The judgment is to support, not to penalise. The failure it prevents: a "welcome back" that becomes an interrogation of whether the absence was real, and a report who never tells you anything again.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: how long they were away, what they have already shared with you, and when and where the meeting can happen privately.
2. Setting: a private, confidential conversation, agreed with them in advance, not ambushed on the first morning.
3. Welcome plan: the first minutes are about them being back, then what changed while they were away (people, priorities, decisions), in plain lines.
4. Questions: open and about the work ("what would make this week manageable?"). I never draft a question about symptoms, diagnosis or whether they were really ill.
5. Ask what they would like colleagues to know, and record their exact answer.
6. Support to consider: flexible working, a phased return, reasonable adjustments, an occupational health referral. Each is marked "check with HR or a qualified adviser"; I do not state what they are entitled to.
7. Agreed note: only what the person agreed to record, shared with them, with a follow-up date.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Return-to-Work Meeting Plan
## Setting
[Where, when, agreed with them on (date)]
## Welcome and What Changed
- [change in people, priorities or decisions]
## Questions
- [open question about the work and what would help]
## Support to Consider
| Option | Check with | Status |
|---|---|---|
| [phased return, flexible hours, adjustment, occupational health] | HR or a qualified adviser | [to raise / agreed] |
## What Stays Confidential
[What they asked to keep private]
## What Colleagues Are Told
[Their words, as agreed]
## Agreed Note
[Only what they agreed to record]
## Decision
[name] confirms the agreed support with HR by [date] and checks in with the person on [date].
```

## Done When
- The meeting is private and agreed in advance
- No question touches symptoms, diagnosis or whether the absence was genuine
- Every support option is marked for HR or a qualified adviser
- The note holds only what the person agreed, and they get a copy

## Quality Bar
- No health or diagnosis detail in the note beyond what the person agrees.
- No absence scoring or counting against trigger points.
- What colleagues hear is the person's choice, in their words.
- Claude prepares the welcome and the questions; it never questions whether an absence was genuine or records health details.
