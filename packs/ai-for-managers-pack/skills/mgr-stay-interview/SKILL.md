# Stay Interview

## When To Use
Good people leave and you did not see it coming. The quiet, reliable ones looked low maintenance until the resignation landed. It answers, before it is too late: what keeps this person here, what would make them go, and what will you change?

## When Not To Use
If you cannot or will not act on anything you hear, do not ask; a stay interview with no follow-up does more harm than none. For growth and next steps use Career Conversation, and for themes from a whole group use Skip-Level Meeting.

## Inputs
- The person's role, how long they have been on the team, and anything they have already said about what they enjoy or find hard (no guesses about their mood)
- What you can actually change: work, schedule, projects, recognition, learning
If you have none of this, I start from the role alone and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
The stay interview, as described in SHRM's public article "How to conduct stay interviews: 5 key questions". Its themes, paraphrased here: what people look forward to, what they are learning and want to learn, why they stay, when they last thought about leaving and why, and what the manager could do differently. The manager listens far more than talks and turns what they hear into a plan. The failure it prevents: the manager asks "are you happy here?", hears "yes, fine", and learns the truth in the exit interview.

## Workflow
1. Ask: how long have they been with you? What have they said about what they enjoy or find hard? What can you genuinely change in the next months?
2. Set it up as its own meeting, never tacked onto a performance review or a 1:1 about a problem. Draft a two-line invitation that says why and that nothing is being evaluated.
3. Question set: one or two open questions per theme (looking forward to, learning, why they stay, last time they thought of leaving, what you could do), in your own words. Put the leaving question in the middle, once trust is there.
4. Probes: after each answer, "give me an example" or "tell me more". Plan to talk for a small share of the time; the user sets how long the meeting runs.
5. Listening notes: in their words, not your interpretation, shared with them afterwards so they can correct them.
6. Stay plan: actions for the manager only, each with a date, split into "can do now" and "will ask about". Strike anything you cannot deliver, and say what you will try, not what you promise.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Stay Interview Plan
**Person:** [name] · **Date:** [date] · **Length:** [set by you]
**Invitation:** [two lines on why, and that nothing is evaluated]
## Question set
| Theme | Question | Probe |
|---|---|---|
| What they look forward to | [open question] | Give me an example |
| Learning, now and wanted | [open question] | Tell me more |
| Why they stay | [open question] | Give me an example |
| Last time they thought of leaving | [open question] | What was happening then? |
| What I could do differently | [open question] | Tell me more |
## Listening notes (their words)
| Theme | What they said |
|---|---|
| [theme] | [notes] |
## Stay plan (manager actions)
| Action | Can do now or will ask about | By when | Told them by |
|---|---|---|---|
| [action] | [now / ask] | [date] | [date] |
## Decision
[manager name] decides which actions to commit to by [date] and tells [name] what will and will not change by [date].
```

## Done When
- The meeting is separate from any performance conversation
- Every theme has an open question and a probe; notes are in the person's words, to be shared
- Every stay plan action belongs to the manager and has a date

## Quality Bar
- Questions are written fresh, not copied from the source article
- No question asks the person to rate their happiness or loyalty
- Nothing promises pay, promotion or changes outside the manager's control, and the notes never feed a performance record
- Claude prepares the questions and the plan; it never scores flight risk or lists who might leave.
