# Claude Graduate Networking Guide: 30 Claude Skills

30 Claude skills for networking from the people you already know to your first job. For final-year students and new graduates.

**Guide and download:** [meet-polar-bear.com/skills/grad-networking-pack](https://meet-polar-bear.com/skills/grad-networking-pack)

## What this is

The pack follows a graduate's networking season, warm people first. You write a brief (the roles you want, the help you need, what you can offer back), then map who you already know from your own LinkedIn data and from real life: lecturers, placement colleagues, part-time managers, society contacts, family friends. You research people and employers from public sources, with a source for every fact, and pick a reason to write that is useful to the other person. Claude gives you points; you write the message and send it. Then it gets you ready for coffee chats and informational interviews, careers fairs and employer events, and ends with introductions, referrals and a light private log that picks your next small run of about 10 people. Each skill is one method or one artifact, with the inputs it needs, where it breaks, a fixed output template, a finish line and the next skill to run.

Every skill holds one line: Claude helps you see who you already know and get ready for every conversation; you choose every person, write every word and send every message yourself, and nothing is scraped, automated, sent for you or invented about you or anyone else.

```
Your brief -> Map who you already know -> Research from public sources -> Write to people -> Coffee chats and informational interviews -> Careers fairs and events -> Introductions, referrals and your log
```

## Install

### In Claude Code

```
/plugin marketplace add polar-bear-org/claude-skills
/plugin install grad-networking-pack@polar-bear-skills
```

### In Claude.ai

1. Turn on "Code execution and file creation" under Settings > Capabilities.
2. Go to Customize > Skills, click "+", choose "+ Create skill", then "Upload a skill", and upload the skill's zip from the `install/` folder. Repeat for each skill you want.
3. On Team and Enterprise plans, an owner first enables "Cloud code execution and file creation" and "Skills" under Organization settings > Plugins & skills (Policy tab), and can upload skills for the whole organization.

Every skill works in a normal chat with what you paste in. Some name an optional Claude surface: a Project (redesigned, beta, select plans) to keep your brief and notes together, Claude Docs (beta) for longer documents, and in the desktop app a Google Sheet created from the chat when Google Drive is connected. Keep contact details out of Claude memory. Claude in Chrome and other browser agents are never used on LinkedIn.

## The skills

### 1 · Your brief

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Networking Brief](skills/gnet-networking-brief/SKILL.md) | Target roles, the help you need, what you can offer back, what you will not ask for yet | You want to "network" but cannot say what for, or who could help |
| [Target Employer List](skills/gnet-target-employer-list/SKILL.md) | 10 to 20 employers against the brief, routes and dates, a sourced reason each, smaller employers, the ones you drop | Your list is five famous names and nothing else |
| [Elevator Pitch](skills/gnet-elevator-pitch/SKILL.md) | A 30-second introduction in your words, a 10-second version, one question to hand over | Someone says "tell me about yourself" and you freeze |

### 2 · Map who you already know

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [LinkedIn Data Export Reader](skills/gnet-linkedin-data-export/SKILL.md) | Download steps, your Connections file as a clean list, message counts per person, gaps noted | You want to start from your real connections, however few |
| [Network Map](skills/gnet-network-map/SKILL.md) | People from real life added by hand, grouped in circles, how each could help, who you forgot | You think you do not know anyone useful |
| [Closeness Tiers](skills/gnet-closeness-tiers/SKILL.md) | Each person tagged in your own words, a first guess you correct, dormant ties flagged | Your list mixes best friends and strangers you accepted once |
| [Contact Shortlist](skills/gnet-contact-shortlist/SKILL.md) | Fit first, closeness second, a cap per employer, about 10 per run, chosen by you | You have a map and do not know who to contact first |

### 3 · Research from public sources

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Alumni Search Plan](skills/gnet-alumni-search-plan/SKILL.md) | Where to look, what to search for, up to 10 names you add by hand, what to ask | Nobody you know works where you want to go |
| [Person Research Notes](skills/gnet-person-research/SKILL.md) | Career path, recent work, shared links, a source per fact, unknowns stated, one question only they could answer | You are about to write to someone and know only their job title |
| [Employer Research Brief](skills/gnet-employer-research/SKILL.md) | What they do, dated news with sources, graduate routes and deadlines, values, three questions a website cannot answer | You need to sound informed at a fair, on a call or in an email |

### 4 · Write to people

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Reason to Write](skills/gnet-reason-to-write/SKILL.md) | One reason per person that helps them, why it fits, what not to ask yet | Every message you start is an ask |
| [Message Points](skills/gnet-message-points/SKILL.md) | 2 or 3 points for your first message, length and ask checked; you write it | You have a reason to write and a blank screen |
| [Connection Request Note](skills/gnet-connection-request-note/SKILL.md) | Points for a short personal note, a check against the default request and any job ask | The default request feels like spam |
| [Speculative Application Email](skills/gnet-speculative-approach/SKILL.md) | Named recipient check, points for each part, follow-up date | You want to work somewhere that is not advertising |
| [Grammar Check](skills/gnet-grammar-check/SKILL.md) | Grammar and spelling fixed only, every change highlighted, flags for a job ask, length, unsourced facts | You wrote it yourself and want it clean and still yours |
| [Follow-Up Nudge](skills/gnet-follow-up-nudge/SKILL.md) | When to nudge, points for one short nudge, when to stop and log it closed | You sent a message and heard nothing |

### 5 · Coffee chats and informational interviews

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Coffee Chat Prep Sheet](skills/gnet-coffee-chat-prep/SKILL.md) | One page: who they are, your pitch, your goal, five question slots, what to offer, how to end on time | Someone said yes to a chat and you have two days |
| [Informational Interview Questions](skills/gnet-informational-interview-questions/SKILL.md) | Questions matched to their stage, none answerable from the website, a closing question on who else to talk to | You do not know what to ask beyond "what is it like" |
| [Practice Conversation](skills/gnet-practice-conversation/SKILL.md) | Claude plays the contact so you rehearse opening, questions and close, then notes what to change | You are nervous about your first informational interview |
| [Chat Debrief](skills/gnet-chat-debrief/SKILL.md) | What you learned, advice to act on, names suggested, promises with dates, one log line | The call just ended and the details are fading |
| [Thank-You Note](skills/gnet-thank-you-note/SKILL.md) | Points for a thank-you that names one piece of advice and what you will do with it | Someone gave you their time and you want them to remember you well |

### 6 · Careers fairs and events

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Careers Fair Plan](skills/gnet-careers-fair-plan/SKILL.md) | Priority and warm-up stands, two questions per employer, your pitch, what to bring, timing, virtual set-up | The fair is next week and you were going to wander round |
| [Employer Event Prep](skills/gnet-event-prep/SKILL.md) | Who is speaking, three questions, one person to meet, how you will introduce yourself and leave | You booked an employer presentation and want more than free pizza |
| [Event Notes and 48-Hour Follow-Up](skills/gnet-event-follow-up/SKILL.md) | Notes right after each conversation, then one follow-up per person within 48 hours | You came home with a stack of names and no plan |

### 7 · Introductions, referrals and your log

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Helper Brief](skills/gnet-helper-brief/SKILL.md) | One page a friend, lecturer or mentor reads to open their own network; they choose and write | Someone says "let me know how I can help" |
| [Introduction Request](skills/gnet-intro-request/SKILL.md) | Points for asking one contact for one introduction, an easy no, double opt-in | A contact mentioned someone you would love to talk to |
| [Forwardable Blurb](skills/gnet-forwardable-blurb/SKILL.md) | Three or four sentences the introducer can forward unchanged | Someone asked you to "send me something I can forward" |
| [Referral Ask](skills/gnet-referral-ask/SKILL.md) | A readiness check first, then points for a referral ask with an easy no | You talked to someone at an employer with an open role |
| [Close the Loop Note](skills/gnet-close-the-loop/SKILL.md) | Points for telling the person who helped what happened, a thank-you, something to share back | The person who helped has heard nothing since |
| [Networking Log](skills/gnet-networking-log/SKILL.md) | A light private log, a weekly review that picks the next run of about 10, archive and delete notes | You cannot remember who you messaged, who replied and what you promised |

## Example prompts

- "Run gnet-networking-brief. Here is my CV. I want a graduate role in [field] and I do not know what to ask anyone for."
- "Here is my LinkedIn Connections file. Clean it into a list and tell me what is missing."
- "I have a careers fair on [date]. Here is the employer list. Help me plan which stands to visit and what to ask."
- "My lecturer said she would introduce me to [contact]. Give me points for a short blurb she can forward."
- "I had a 15-minute call with an alumna. Here are my rough notes. Debrief it and tell me what I promised."
- "I sent a message ten days ago and heard nothing. Should I nudge, and what would I say?"

## Quality bar

- **One method, applied properly.** Each skill uses the real steps of its source (careers guidance on fairs, informational interviews and speculative approaches, double opt-in introductions, the LinkedIn data download), not a generic "gather, analyse, recommend".
- **Points, not text.** For every message, Claude gives points drawn from your notes and sourced research. You write the message; the grammar check fixes grammar only and shows every change.
- **A source for every fact.** Research on people and employers comes from public sources, each fact with its source. Unknowns stay unknown and a thin profile is never padded.
- **Nobody is scored.** Tiers describe how well you know someone, never their worth. Fit is about roles and employers against your brief. You pick every name, in small runs of about 10.
- **Private by default.** Nothing is fetched from LinkedIn, contact details stay out of Claude memory and are never passed to helpers, and the log keeps only what you need. Data protection questions end with "check with a qualified adviser".
- **The red line:** Claude helps you see who you already know and get ready for every conversation; you choose every person, write every word and send every message yourself, and nothing is scraped, automated, sent for you or invented about you or anyone else.

## Where it comes from

The pack is research-informed. The roster comes from the artifacts graduates are told to prepare by UK careers guidance and from a scan of what final-year students and new graduates say they struggle with when networking. Each method is attributed through a public source: Prospects and TARGETjobs guidance, UK university careers services, LinkedIn's own help and user agreement, the UK Information Commissioner's Office, a research paper on dormant ties and an originator post on double opt-in introductions. Sources and their limits are in `resources/evidence-and-sources.md`.
