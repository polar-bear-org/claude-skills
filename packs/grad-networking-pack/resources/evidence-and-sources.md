# Evidence and sources

Research checked 4 October 2026.

## What this pack claims, and what it does not

The pack claims that each skill applies a documented practice faithfully, attributes it to a public source, and states where it breaks. It claims that the order of the pack and the "When to run it" lines come from what final-year students and new graduates say they struggle with when networking, and from what UK careers guidance tells them to do.

It does not claim that using these skills gets anyone a conversation, an introduction, a referral, an interview or a job. No source below tested these skills. Most sources are careers guidance, platform help pages or an originator's description of a method, not studies of its effect. This is a practice resource, not a validated intervention. Nothing in the pack is legal advice: platform rules and data protection points are framed as questions, and end with "check with a qualified adviser".

## How the struggles scan was done

The pack reuses an earlier scan of graduate job-search struggles (web pages grouped into clusters, plus a small Reddit scan treated as a weak signal), and adds a pass on 4 October 2026 aimed at networking: UK careers guidance pages, platform help pages, the regulator's guidance and one anonymous professional forum snippet. Each statement was paraphrased in under 20 words and no usernames were stored. The largest struggles were "cold outreach rarely leads anywhere", "I don't know anyone", "not knowing what to say", "careers fairs that lead nowhere" and "what to ask in an informational interview and how to follow up". Limits: much of the new evidence is guidance describing what students get wrong rather than students describing it themselves; six targeted searches found no first-person posts in UK graduate job communities; "findings", "pages" and "items" are different units. Counts show emphasis in what we collected, not how common a struggle is. The struggles set the order of the pack and the wording of its "When" lines; they did not remove any method.

## Methods described generically (no originator cited)

Some steps have no public originator page, or their only source is a book. The pack describes them in its own words and names no author: write a brief before looking at people; closeness described in your own words from real exchange; fit to the brief first and closeness second, with a cap per employer; small runs of about 10 people; a source for every fact; reasons to write that help the other person; points rather than ready-made text; a grammar-only edit that shows each change; short, about-them messages with one easy question; questions about trends, the person's path, advice and resources; a forwardable blurb; rehearsal role-play as used in careers services' mock practice.

## Deliberate design choices

These are Polar Bear's choices, not findings from the sources. The red line ("Claude helps you see who you already know and get ready for every conversation; you choose every person, write every word and send every message yourself, and nothing is scraped, automated, sent for you or invented about you or anyone else") is a position we take; no source above tested it. The pack is warm-first because the struggles scan and the careers guidance both point to people you already know, not because a study proved it works better for graduates. Nothing scores, ranks or profiles people: closeness tiers describe how well you know someone, never their worth; fit compares roles and employers with your brief; the graduate picks every name. Claude gives points and a grammar-only check; it never writes or sends the message. Nothing is fetched from LinkedIn: the pack works from the graduate's own data download and people added by hand, and browser agents are never used on LinkedIn. Contact details stay out of Claude memory and are never passed to helpers or introducers. Data protection points are questions that end with "check with a qualified adviser", never advice. The practice conversation is rehearsal only and is never used during a real call. The pack names the reader's role only, and never names a university or employer from anyone's network data. The "When Not To Use" lines draw on each source's own caveats where one exists; where none exists, the limit is our practitioner judgment and is written as such.
