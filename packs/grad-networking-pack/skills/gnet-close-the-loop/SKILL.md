# Close the Loop Note

## When To Use
Someone introduced you or referred you, the conversation happened or the application went in, and they have heard nothing since. They opened a door and are now wondering whether it was worth it. This gives you points for telling them what happened, thanking them and sharing something back.

## When Not To Use
If you want to thank the person you actually spoke to, use Thank-You Note: that is a different message to a different person. If nothing has happened yet because the introduction is still pending, wait; a nudge is Follow-Up Nudge.

## Inputs
- Who helped and what they did (an introduction, a referral, a helper brief passed on)
- What happened since: the call, the advice, the application, any outcome
- Your Chat Debrief notes, if you have them
If you have none of this, I start from who helped and what happened, in a sentence each, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
UK university careers guidance says to thank people and share outcomes with those who helped. The judgment is that an introducer's only reward is knowing it went somewhere. The failure it prevents: the referrer hearing from the hiring team, months later, that you got the job, and wondering why you never said. A short, honest note, even "no news yet", keeps the door open.

## Workflow
1. Ask up to three questions: who helped and how, what happened, and what you are doing next.
2. Draft the point on what happened: the call was held, the advice you got, the outcome so far. State it as it is, including "no news yet" or "I did not get it".
3. Draft the point on thanks: name the specific thing they did and why it mattered to you.
4. Draft the point on something to share back: an article, a link, a result of their advice, or an offer to help someone they know later.
5. Check what you pass on about the third person: their advice in general terms is fine; anything they said in confidence or about their own life stays out.
6. Check the points: 2 or 3 points, each 3 or 4 sentences, short, no new ask unless it is tiny. You write the note and send it.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Close the Loop Note Points
**To:** [helper's first name] · **What they did:** [introduction / referral / passed on brief] · **Date:** [date]
## Points to make
1. **What happened:** [3 or 4 sentences: call held, advice received, outcome so far, stated as it is]
2. **Thanks:** [3 or 4 sentences naming what they did]
3. **Something back:** [3 or 4 sentences: a link, a result, an offer]
## Left out on purpose
- [anything private about the person you spoke to]
## Decision
You decide the wording and send it by [date]; you log it in your Networking Log the same day.
```

## Done When
- The outcome is stated as it really is, including "no news yet"
- Thanks name the specific thing the helper did
- Nothing private about the third person is in the points
- At most one tiny ask, or none

## Quality Bar
- Points, not a finished message; if asked to write it, offer Grammar Check on your draft instead
- Never inflate an outcome to make the helper feel better
- Never pass on anything said in confidence
- Short beats complete; the helper wants the headline
- You report what really happened in your own words.
