# Practice lab

All examples below are fictional. They illustrate outputs, not evidence of results or predictions about real people.

## 1. Missed handoffs without a character judgment
**Situation:** A 45-person agency's project lead has missed two agreed handoff dates. The founder knows the dates, but not why they were missed.

**Useful skill:** Underperformance Support, followed by a rehearsal if wanted.

**Opening:** “The research handoffs on 4 and 11 May arrived a day after the dates we agreed. That left the design team less time to prepare. I want to understand what got in the way and agree a plan we can deliver.”

**Questions:** Were the dates and inputs realistic? Which dependency slipped? What could the founder change?

**Draft for discussion:** One agreed handoff checkpoint; an early flag when inputs are missing; the founder resolves conflicting priorities; both review the next two handoffs. The result is a proposal until discussed. No attitude label or invented performance rating is needed.

## 2. Care that changes a deadline
**Situation:** An eight-person delivery team has about 40 hours available for 60 hours of commitments next week. Two people report regularly working evenings.

**Useful skill:** Workload and Boundaries.

**Decision for the founder:** Renegotiate 20 hours of scope or provide genuinely available capacity. “Everyone should protect their time” leaves the same impossible equation.

**Client opening:** “We can deliver the agreed priority flow by Friday. Completing the additional variants to the same standard requires either a later date or reducing the other scope. Here are the two workable options.”

**Follow-through:** Name the person negotiating the trade-off, protect the team from unapproved extras, and review demand and capacity next week. These numbers are supplied scenario facts, not AI estimates of employee productivity.

## 3. Supporting someone after a loss
**Situation:** A colleague privately tells a manager that a close friend has died and asks for space. Their leave policy has not yet been checked.

**Useful skill:** Support Through Loss.

**First message:** “I'm sorry to hear this. I can cover today's client update. There's no need to explain more. When you feel able, let me know how you'd prefer us to keep in touch and whether you want anything shared with the team.”

**Manager actions:** Arrange cover now, check available leave/flexibility through the appropriate person, and ask before sharing the reason for absence. Do not announce a fixed return date, promise unconfirmed leave, or require the colleague to describe their grief.

## A 35-minute team practice session
For managers learning together; use fictional cases, not live employee cases. Participation and role-play are optional.

1. **5 minutes — Choose the work:** Each person picks a fictional scenario. Agree that observations concern wording and actions, not personalities.
2. **8 minutes — Prepare:** Use one skill to create a brief opening and a next step. Mark unknowns.
3. **10 minutes — Practice:** In pairs, rehearse one turn at a time. Offer silent drafting as an alternative. Either person can pause. The counterpart is fictional.
4. **7 minutes — Debrief:** What was clear? Where did the wording assume intent? What would the leader actually need to change or authorize?
5. **5 minutes — Commit:** Each person chooses one behavior to try. Arrange a voluntary follow-up; do not collect personal compassion scores.

Keep real allegations, medical details, and formal employment cases out of a group exercise. Use appropriate private processes for those situations.
