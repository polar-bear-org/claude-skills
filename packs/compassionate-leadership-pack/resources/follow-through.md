# Turn a useful conversation into action

## A minimal action note
Save this only if useful and appropriate. Use role labels or aliases. Keep private health information, allegations, and speculative motives out of a general team note.

- **Situation / date:**
- **What we know:** observable facts, with disputed points marked.
- **What is still a proposal:**
- **What the authorized people agreed:**
- **Action / owner / due date:**
- **Support or resources committed:**
- **What will be shared, with whom, and whose permission is needed:**
- **Next check-in:**
- **What would make us change or stop this plan:**

If no agreement has been reached, write that plainly. A draft is not a record of consent. Sensitive formal records belong in the organization's approved process.

## A two-week practice plan
**Day 1:** Choose one real situation and one skill. Use an alias. Prepare an opening or a work decision; review it before use.

**Days 2–4:** Have the human conversation. Ask for the other person's perspective. Update the draft against what was actually said. Make one concrete commitment you have authority to keep.

**End of week 1:** Check whether the action happened. If workload or authority blocked it, fix that condition rather than sending a more insistent reminder.

**Week 2:** Use Leadership Retrospective on the episode. Ask for feedback where appropriate, with an easy way to decline. Keep one behavior to practice again.

**End of week 2:** If the same issue recurs, use Workplace Pattern Review. Choose a small operating change and a review date.

This is a suggested rhythm, not a prescribed improvement program. Do not measure success by whether employees praise the leader. Look for completed commitments, clearer decisions, and fewer repeated work problems, while remaining open to contrary feedback.
