# Claude for Networking on LinkedIn: 31 Claude Skills

31 Claude skills for winning clients through the people you already know, from your own network to warm introductions. For consultants, advisers, fractional executives, founders and owners who sell their own expertise.

**Guide and download:** [meet-polar-bear.com/skills/networking-on-linkedin-pack](https://meet-polar-bear.com/skills/networking-on-linkedin-pack)

## What this is

The pack follows one method in the order you do the work. You write your brief first (who you help, who you want to meet, what a good introduction looks like). You map your own network from your own LinkedIn data export: fit to the brief first, closeness second, in tiers you set in your own words, and a shortlist you choose by hand. You research each person from public sources with a source for every fact, pick a reason to write that is useful to them, and write the message yourself. Then come the parts of networking that usually slip: past clients you have not spoken to in a year, the specific ask, the introduction that never gets written, the partner who keeps sending work, and a weekly rhythm of small runs that brings you back to the brief each month. Where a skill fits a Claude surface (a private Claude Project, memory, Claude Docs, Claude in Chrome, the Gmail and Google Calendar connectors), it says so, and beta features are marked beta.

Every skill holds one line: Claude helps you see your own network and prepare each conversation; you choose every person, write every word and send every message yourself. Nothing is scraped, automated or sent for you.

```
Write your brief -> Map your own network -> Research and prepare -> Write for a reason -> Tend past clients -> Ask and introduce -> Keep the rhythm
```

## The skills

### 1 · Write your brief

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Networking Brief](skills/net-networking-brief/SKILL.md) | Who you help, the problem you solve, what you want help with now, who you would like to meet, a good introduction, who is not a fit | People like you but nobody can say who to send your way |
| [Ideal Client Profile](skills/net-ideal-client-profile/SKILL.md) | Fit criteria as situations, roles and triggers, signals you can read in a connections list, poor-fit signs, fit questions | "Anyone who needs help with X" is your only answer to "who should I introduce you to" |
| [Elevator Pitch](skills/net-elevator-pitch/SKILL.md) | One or two sentences a contact can repeat, the problem in the client's words, three versions to test, words to drop | People you are introduced to rule you out before a call because nobody could explain what you do |
| [Five-Minute Favor List](skills/net-five-minute-favor-list/SKILL.md) | What you can give in five minutes, who it suits by situation, what you will not offer, a log with no scorekeeping | You want to reach out but feel you have nothing to offer except an ask |

### 2 · Map your own network

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [LinkedIn Data Export Guide](skills/net-linkedin-data-export/SKILL.md) | Request steps from LinkedIn's help page, which files to use and leave out, where the file lives, column cleanup, when to delete it | You want to see your whole network without scraping or a tool that logs in as you |
| [Network Fit Map](skills/net-network-fit-map/SKILL.md) | Each connection read against the brief, the visible match and its reason, fit left for you to set, a fit floor, a cap per company | You have hundreds of connections and no idea who is relevant to what you do now |
| [Closeness Tiers](skills/net-closeness-tiers/SKILL.md) | A first estimate from your own message exchanges, four tiers in your own words, every tier corrected by you | You cannot tell who you could actually write to and who would not remember you |
| [Dormant Ties List](skills/net-dormant-ties-list/SKILL.md) | People you once exchanged with often and lost touch with, the thread to pick up, what to research, the reason to reconnect | You only reach out when you need work, so most of your network has gone quiet |
| [Hand-Picked Shortlist](skills/net-hand-picked-shortlist/SKILL.md) | About 10 names you choose, your reason for each, fit and tier as you set them, company cap checked, names set aside | The map is done and you need to decide who you will actually write to this week |

### 3 · Research and prepare

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Person Research Brief](skills/net-person-research-brief/SKILL.md) | Public facts about one person's work, each with a source and date, unknowns stated, what you share, open questions | You want to write something specific and refuse to guess or pad |
| [Trigger Event Scan](skills/net-trigger-event-scan/SKILL.md) | Recent public events with date and source, which one is worth a congratulation, what is too old or too private | You want a real reason to write now, not "just checking in" |
| [Coffee Chat Prep](skills/net-coffee-chat-prep/SKILL.md) | What you know (sourced), three questions you want answered, one thing to offer, what you will not pitch, how to close | Someone said yes to a call and you want it to feel like a conversation, not a sales meeting |

### 4 · Write for a reason

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Reason to Write Picker](skills/net-reason-to-write/SKILL.md) | The five reasons, which fits this person, why it is useful to them, the reasons that would be a pitch in disguise | You know who to write to and not what to say that is not an ask |
| [Talking Points](skills/net-talking-points/SKILL.md) | 2 or 3 sourced points for the chosen reason, nothing pushed, no message assembled | You want help thinking about what to say, not a message that sounds like AI wrote it |
| [Message Checklist](skills/net-message-checklist/SKILL.md) | Your own message checked line by line against the checklist, flags only, no rewriting | You wrote the message yourself and want a second look before you send it |
| [Grammar Check](skills/net-grammar-check/SKILL.md) | Grammar, spelling and punctuation fixes only, each change shown before and after, tone and length untouched | You want the message clean without it stopping sounding like you |

### 5 · Tend past clients

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Past Client Reconnect List](skills/net-past-client-list/SKILL.md) | Past clients from your own records, last real exchange, what has changed, the thread to pick up, no ranking by value | Your easiest next project is with someone you already worked with, and you have not spoken in a year |
| [Client Check-In](skills/net-client-check-in/SKILL.md) | A no-ask conversation plan, what changed, one useful thing to bring, questions, what you will not sell, a note to log | Clients only hear from you when there is a renewal or an invoice |
| [Client After-Action Review](skills/net-client-after-action-review/SKILL.md) | With the client: what was meant to happen, what happened, why, what to keep and change, what they want next | A project just ended and you are about to move on without learning or asking anything |
| [Client Case Study](skills/net-client-case-study/SKILL.md) | A short case from an interview, the client's approval of naming and numbers, a sourced figure list, a one-page version | You want something worth sharing with a past client and their peers that they have approved |
| [Client Relationship Map](skills/net-client-relationship-map/SKILL.md) | Who you know inside a client, two-way dependencies, who else faces the problem, facts separate from guesses | You know one person at a client and the work could help a second team you have never met |

### 6 · Ask and introduce

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Introduction Ask](skills/net-introduction-ask/SKILL.md) | When to ask, one specific introduction tied to the brief, the easy way to say no, what you offer back, what not to say | Asking for introductions feels needy, and "let me know if you hear of anyone" has never produced a name |
| [Forwardable Intro Blurb](skills/net-forwardable-blurb/SKILL.md) | A short note the introducer can forward unedited: who you are, why this person, what you would ask, how to decline | Someone agreed to introduce you and the introduction has not happened, because they have to write it |
| [Double Opt-In Introduction](skills/net-double-opt-in-intro/SKILL.md) | Two separate permission notes, what each side gains, the introduction once both say yes, a graceful line for a no | You want to connect two people without putting either on the spot |
| [Shared Introduction List](skills/net-shared-intro-list/SKILL.md) | Your brief shared with a friend or partner, their people added against it, an owner column, what stays private, how to retire it | A friend or partner offers to help and you need a way they can add people without you seeing their contacts |
| [Partner Agreement](skills/net-partner-agreement/SKILL.md) | Each side's work, who each introduces to the other and why, how introductions and loop closing work, fee questions for an adviser, review date | Another firm keeps sending you work, or could, and nothing about it is written down |
| [Thank-You Note](skills/net-thank-you-note/SKILL.md) | A thank-you within days, what happened next (only what may be shared), a give back, a reminder to update the introducer | Someone introduced you and you never told them how it went |

### 7 · Keep the rhythm

| Skill | What it does | When to run it |
|---|---|---|
| [Run of Ten Plan](skills/net-run-of-ten/SKILL.md) | This week's run of about 10 people, reason and status per person, what is ready, what waits for research | You network in bursts when work dries up, then stop when it returns |
| [Follow-Up Tracker](skills/net-follow-up-tracker/SKILL.md) | Replies due, one polite follow-up and when, when to stop, times found in your calendar, threads found in your inbox | Good conversations die because nobody wrote back after the first reply |
| [Personal CRM Log](skills/net-personal-crm-log/SKILL.md) | Who you spoke to and when, what they need, what you promised and by when, next touch date, facts only | You promised an introduction three weeks ago and cannot remember to whom |
| [Monthly Network Review](skills/net-monthly-network-review/SKILL.md) | Where introductions and work came from, which reasons got replies, promises kept and open, gaps, the brief updated | You cannot tell whether the network is working until the pipeline is empty |

## Quality bar

- **One method, applied properly.** Each skill carries the real mechanics of its method (the tiers, the order of the pass, the two permission notes, the review questions), not a generic "gather, analyse, recommend".
- **It tells you where it breaks.** Every skill says when not to use it and which skill in the pack fits instead.
- **Same shape every time.** A fixed output template and a short "done when" check before handover.
- **No invented facts.** No made-up quotes, results, numbers or details about anyone. Every fact about a person has a public source and a date; what is unknown stays unknown.
- **Nobody is ranked.** Fit is to your brief, closeness is how well you know someone, and you set both in your own words. No skill scores, grades or profiles a person, and contact details and messages never go into a shared file.
- **The red line:** Claude helps you see your own network and prepare each conversation; you choose every person, write every word and send every message yourself. Nothing is scraped, automated or sent for you.

## Where it comes from

The pack is research-informed. Its order follows a networking method described generically in the pack, opened by the struggle people who sell their own expertise name most: work that depends on a few people sending it, with nothing in place when they stop. Each named method is attributed to its originator through a public source (the double opt-in introduction, dormant ties, weak ties, the after-action review, research on how introductions happen, LinkedIn's own help pages). Sources and their limits are in `resources/evidence-and-sources.md`.

Cited sources do not imply endorsement. This is a practice resource, not a validated intervention.
