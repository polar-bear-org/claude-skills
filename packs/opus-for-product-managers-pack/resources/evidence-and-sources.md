# Evidence and sources

Research checked 3 October 2026. Method sources rechecked at build on 4 October 2026.

## What this pack claims, and what it does not

The pack claims three things. Each skill applies a named method faithfully, attributes it to its originator through a public source, and says where it breaks. Every Claude feature a skill relies on (Projects, memory, skills, connectors, scheduled tasks, Claude Docs, Claude Slides, Claude Design, Claude Code) was read on an official Anthropic page, and beta features are marked as beta. The order of the phases and the "When to run it" lines come from what product managers, product owners and heads of product say they struggle with.

It does not claim that these skills make a product succeed, save a given number of hours or improve any metric. No source below tested these skills. Most sources describe a method or a product feature; they are not studies of its effect. Claude features change often: connector pages, plan availability and beta limits can differ from what is written here, so each skill tells you to re-check the official page before you rely on a feature. This is a practice resource, not a validated intervention.

## How the struggles scan was done

The pack reuses an earlier scan of 176 public pain statements from two product management communities on Reddit, LinkedIn posts from the previous three months and question-shaped Google search terms, collected in late September 2026, paraphrased in under 20 words each, with no usernames stored, and grouped into 14 struggles. A web pass on 3 October 2026 added newsletters, discussion threads, surveys and blogs about decks, status updates, specs and working with AI, plus five new pains specific to working with Claude: no product context, tools not connected, security blocks, low trust in output, and AI used only on the easy half of the job. Limits: Reddit could not be reached in the new pass; several new sources are vendor blogs or surveys; some are older than twelve months; and relevance search is not a random sample. Counts show emphasis in what we pulled, not how common a struggle is across the profession. The struggles set the order of the pack and the wording of its "When" lines; they did not remove any method.

## Deliberate design choices

These are Polar Bear's choices, not findings from the sources. The red line ("Claude reads your tools, drafts the work and runs the routines; you talk to the customers, make every call, and press send") is a position we take; no source above tested it. Every skill ends with a decision that a named role makes, and no skill or routine sends a message, posts, files a ticket or changes a record on its own: connectors stay read only unless you turn a write on for one task, and scheduled routines only draft. Nothing scores, ranks or profiles people: the skills score options, risks, hypotheses and work; stakeholders are mapped by role and decision right, never rated; customer evidence is reported as themes and aggregates, small groups are merged, and customer personal data is kept out of memory, the context file and decks. Legal, regulatory, privacy and security questions are flagged "check with a qualified adviser" rather than answered. The pack names the reader's role only and says nothing about the size or kind of organisation they work in. The "When Not To Use" lines draw on each originator's own caveats where one exists; where none exists, the limit is our practitioner judgment and is written as such.
