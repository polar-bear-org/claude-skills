# Project Interview

## When To Use
You did a real project, a dissertation or a placement task, and every time you try to describe it you get stuck at "we did a presentation and got a good mark". This interview gets underneath that line, one question at a time, and answers one question: what actually happened, and which part of it was yours?

## When Not To Use
If you want a quick list of everything you have done across your degree, jobs and societies, run Experience Inventory first; this skill goes deep on one project only. If the project is still running or not yet marked, wait: moments 5 and 6 need an ending.

## Inputs
- The project in one line, and who else worked on it (by role: teammates, tutor, placement manager)
- Any rules on sharing it: module rules, the employer's confidentiality or social media policy
- Optional: the brief, your feedback sheet, emails, slides, your Experience Inventory row for it
If you have none of this, I start from your one-line description and your memory, and mark every fact "memory only".

## Approach
The six-moment interview is a Polar Bear practice method, adapted here from client projects to a module, a tutor, a group or a placement manager, with the STAR habit of "I, not we" from the National Careers Service guide to the STAR method. The order gets the real story out before the polished one, and I write down what you say without improving it. The failure it prevents: a write-up that says "we delivered a successful report" and could be anyone's, or one where a teammate's analysis quietly becomes yours. Plain chat works; a Project (beta, select plans) keeps the notes beside your Experience Inventory.

## Workflow
1. Two questions before the story: the project in one line, and who else worked on it plus any rules on sharing it (module rules, employer confidentiality). I record exactly what you say about permission, not what you hope.
2. Moment 1, what was actually wrong or hard: not the brief, the thing behind it. Follow-up: how did you find out? Then moment 2, the moment you understood it. Follow-up: what did you notice that you had missed before?
3. Moment 3, the turn: the decision or piece of work that changed direction. Follow-up: who made the call? If it was the group or the tutor, the notes say so.
4. Moment 4, the mistake or dead end. Follow-up: what did you do next? People skip this one, and it is the one a reader believes. I wait for a real answer before moving on.
5. Moment 5, what you delivered and the first time it was used or assessed; moment 6, what changed and how you know. One follow-up each. I ask about concrete moments ("what was on the whiteboard", "what did the first email after say"), never "what was the outcome".
6. Mark every fact "record" (with the source: feedback sheet, email, mark) or "memory only". A number without a record stays words. Your words stay verbatim, including the clumsy ones.
7. Build the do-not-claim list with you: other people's parts, results you hoped for and did not see, anything confidential or not yet marked. Stop at about 20 minutes; past that people start performing for the record.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Project Interview Notes
Project: [one line] · Dates: [from to] · With: [roles]
Sharing rules: [module rules, employer policy, as you stated them]
## The six moments
| Moment | Your words | Record or memory | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 What was wrong | [verbatim] | [record / memory only] | [feedback sheet, email] |
| 2 When you understood it | [verbatim] | [ ] | [ ] |
| 3 The turn (who decided) | [verbatim] | [ ] | [ ] |
| 4 The mistake and what next | [verbatim] | [ ] | [ ] |
| 5 What you delivered, first use | [verbatim] | [ ] | [ ] |
| 6 What changed, how you know | [verbatim] | [ ] | [ ] |
## Your part
[What was yours, in "I" sentences; what was the group's, by role]
## Do-not-claim list
- [Other people's parts] · [Hoped-for results] · [Confidential or unmarked]
## Decision
You decide by [date] whether these notes are ready for a case study, or which moment needs a record first.
```

## Done When
- All six moments have an answer in your words, with moment 4 not left blank or softened.
- Every fact is marked record or memory only; no number sits without a source.
- The do-not-claim list has at least one entry or says "checked, none".
- Your part and the group's part are separated, with teammates by role.

## Quality Bar
- One question at a time, one follow-up each; never a form to fill.
- Nothing is polished or summarised at this stage; the notes are a record.
- No judgement of anyone's contribution: "we split the work differently", never "they did nothing".
- Quotes from a tutor or manager only if you are sure of the wording and the person knows; otherwise a paraphrase without quotation marks.
- Your words are recorded as said; the do-not-claim list travels with every draft.
