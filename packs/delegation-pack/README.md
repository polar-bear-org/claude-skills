# Delegation Pack

**Ten practical AI skills to hand over useful work with clear outcomes, real authority, and enough support.** For founders and managers in agencies and professional-services teams of roughly 20–200.

Use this pack when you keep doing everything yourself, a handoff keeps bouncing back, or delegated responsibility is becoming extra work without the power to act. You leave with briefs, capacity decisions, decision maps, conversation guides, and recovery plans. People agree the work and make the decisions.

## Start in five minutes

1. Pick one real task and read the relevant prompt below.
2. Paste its `portable/` file into your assistant, then describe the situation with aliases.
3. Give your initial view first. Let AI organize the facts and challenge assumptions.
4. Verify the draft with the person doing the work before treating it as an agreement.

Example: “Use delegation-authority-map. My account lead will run a client workshop. They can choose the format, but I retain pricing and contract changes. Help me make the boundaries usable.”

## Use with your assistant

**Any text assistant:** paste an individual file from `portable/` into a new conversation. Every prompt is self-contained. AI tools vary in context capacity and instruction following; this is prompt portability, not a native integration guarantee.

**A project:** add `portable/ALL-SKILLS.md` as a source and use `PROJECT-INSTRUCTIONS.md` for routing. If retrieval is unreliable, paste the individual prompt directly. Keep sensitive employee records out of shared projects.

**Agent Skills tools:** copy selected folders from `skills/` to the location supported by your assistant. The pack includes a Claude plugin manifest. Marketplace availability begins after publication; this review copy is not registered as a live plugin.

## Keep judgment human

Autonomy can help, and it can create strain when responsibility exceeds capacity or authority. These skills make those conditions discussable. They draft work agreements; they do not assign tasks, judge employees, send messages, or replace workplace policy. Emergency and reserved decisions stay with the appropriate human decision maker.

The research supports design choices, with considerable observational and context-specific evidence. This pack itself is not a validated intervention and promises no measured performance gain.
