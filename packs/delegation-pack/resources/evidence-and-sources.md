# Evidence and sources

Research checked 10 September 2026. These are original practical workflows informed by research, not a tested intervention. A citation supports the limited finding described here; it does not validate the whole skill. Meta-analyses aggregate different tasks, populations, and measures. Associations and statistical mediation do not establish a causal chain. Do not promise a percentage improvement from using this pack.

## What the evidence changes in the design

We pair discretion with usable authority, capacity, support, and an agreed outcome. We make checkpoints contingent on the work and keep legitimate requests for help open. We treat a learning investment as a possible early cost. We avoid a universal delegation ladder, personality-based assignment, blanket rules against taking work back, or the claim that more autonomy is always better. Templates, categories, dialogue, and review cadences are Polar Bear design choices.

## How to use the evidence responsibly

Start with a small real task. Ask the owner whether the brief, discretion, workload, and support are workable. Review quality against agreed criteria, time spent by both parties, avoidable rework, and the owner's experience. Improve the next handoff from that local feedback; changes in a small uncontrolled trial are not proof of efficacy. Do not score or rank employees using these prompts.
