# Weekly LinkedIn Routine

## When To Use
You sell while delivering, and LinkedIn is the first thing to fall off the week. Use this once a week, ideally the day before your blocked hour, to answer: what has Claude already got ready, and what are the few things only I can do in sixty minutes?

## When Not To Use
If you have not decided what goes out this month, run Content Calendar first; this skill runs one week, it does not plan the month. In a week with no hour at all, apply your skipped-week rule and stop; a rushed post does more harm than a quiet week.

## Inputs
- This week's slot from your Content Calendar, and the Idea Bank entry behind it.
- The threads and call notes you choose from this week; with the Gmail connector I search and read only those, never send.
- Your Personal Voice Guide and do-not-claim list.
- Optional: Google Calendar (listed in the official sales plugin connectors) to find the hour, and a Project (beta) holding the files above.
If you have none of this, I start from the one idea you want to post this week and mark the routine as a first draft.

## Approach
A weekly review routine (a common practice, no single originator) works because the thinking is done before the hour starts, so the hour is spent on the parts that need you. LinkedIn's Professional Community Policies set the rules column: authentic engagement, no automation, no pods. The failure this prevents is opening LinkedIn "to post something", scrolling for forty minutes and closing it with nothing written and no one contacted.

## Workflow
1. Ask three questions: when is your hour this week, which threads or call notes may I read, and how do you want the sixty minutes split across the blocks.
2. Before you sit down, I prepare: two or three candidate ideas from the threads you chose, each with its source; the week's draft from the calendar idea; the AI Tells Check and the Claim and Permission Check on that draft. I never prepare comment text or messages.
3. Block one, the draft: you edit it in your voice and resolve every flagged claim. A claim you cannot back comes out, even if the post gets shorter.
4. Block two, comments: on posts you chose, by people whose work you follow, you write two or three comments with something real to add. No pods, no comment swaps, nothing scheduled.
5. Block three, conversations: from your Warm Conversations List, you write and send one to three notes by hand. Warm before cold.
6. Last five minutes: you post, then add one line per conversation started to your Conversations Log. If time ran out, the draft waits; the comments and notes still count.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Weekly LinkedIn Routine
Week of: [date]  |  Hour: [day and time]

## Ready before you sit down (Claude)
- Candidate ideas: [idea, source] / [idea, source]
- Draft: [title], from Idea Bank entry [idea]
- Checks: AI tells [n flags], claims [n unbacked]

## The hour (you)
| Block | Minutes (yours) | What you do | Done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Draft | [your number] | Edit in your voice, clear every flagged claim, post | [ ] |
| Comments | [your number] | Comment on posts you chose, with something real to add | [ ] |
| Conversations | [your number] | Write and send notes from your Warm Conversations List | [ ] |
| Log | [your number] | One line per conversation in the Conversations Log | [ ] |
Rules: no pods, no scheduled comments, no automation tools, no bulk messages.

## Decision
[You confirm the hour and the draft by [day before the hour]; if the week falls apart, you apply your skipped-week rule.]
```

## Done When
- Everything Claude prepared is marked as prepared, with its source, and every comment, note and post sits in a block you do yourself.
- The hour adds up to sixty minutes in your own split.
- The routine ends with the Conversations Log, not with a look at likes.

## Quality Bar
- Ideas come only from threads and notes you chose; I read nothing else.
- No comment or message text is drafted for you; comments and notes start with you.
- The rules column follows LinkedIn's policies; anything that looks like engagement bait is cut.
- A short week shrinks the draft block, never the checks.
- Claude prepares; you post, comment and message.
