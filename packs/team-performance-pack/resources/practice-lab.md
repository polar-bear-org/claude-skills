# Team Performance Practice Lab

All cases and outputs below are fictional authored examples, not model-run test results or evidence that the pack works. Practice with aliases and let people opt out of sensitive disclosure.

## Case 1 — Repeated creative rework

**Input:** A seven-person agency team delivered ten concepts last cycle. Four were returned because account staff and designers interpreted acceptance differently. The deadline was unchanged; client revisions may also explain some rework. Run team-performance-handoff then team-performance-experiment.

**Expected worked output:**

- Handoff trigger: account lead submits a concept brief to the design lead.
- Minimum package: approved objective, target audience, mandatory elements, current version, unresolved client questions and named acceptance owner.
- Acknowledgement: receiving lead confirms receipt within the agreed working-day window. Acceptance is separate: either ready to start or missing requirements returned with specific questions.
- Exception: urgent incomplete brief requires the delivery owner to choose reduced scope or revised timing; the designer does not silently absorb cleanup.
- Trial: next ten comparable briefs. Baseline is four acceptance-related returns out of ten; outcome is returns/briefs, process is packages complete at first receipt, balancing measure is preparation minutes. Record client scope changes separately.
- Review: account and design leads check actual results at the end of that batch. Keep/adapt/stop remains undecided until data exists.

**Checks:** Does the model preserve client change as a rival explanation? Does it avoid inventing a trial improvement? Is acceptance distinct from receipt? Does added administration have a guardrail?

## Case 2 — A remote meeting without decisions

**Input:** Eight colleagues across Toronto and Prague spend 45 minutes twice weekly on a status meeting. Last meeting had no documented decision; one blocked dependency requires discussion. Two colleagues cannot reasonably attend the current slot. Run team-performance-meeting-reset and team-performance-norms.

**Expected worked output:**

- Replace routine status narration with a short written update: change since last update, dependency, help required and needed-by date.
- Keep a 20-minute coordination meeting for the one blocked dependency, with its sender, receiver and decision owner. Include affected absent colleagues' input before the decision.
- Agenda: 3 minutes facts; 7 options/dependency constraints; 5 decision; 5 owners and confirmation. Timing is a proposed convenience.
- Draft norm: routine updates are reviewed within a jointly agreed working-hours window; genuine urgency uses named coverage. Nonresponse does not approve scope changes.
- Trial: two meetings. Check whether required coordination decisions were made, total participant minutes, missing perspectives and displaced writing time. Do not assume remote participation has identical effects to in-person research.

**Checks:** Is the output concrete enough to run? Are time zones and writing burden included? Does it avoid promising faster delivery from fewer meetings?

## Case 3 — Sensitive allegations in a retro (edge case)

**Input:** A manager asks for a retrospective where everybody must identify “who caused the missed deadline.” One team member has privately raised retaliation concerns. The team also has ordinary version-control confusion. Run team-performance-retro.

**Expected worked output:**

- Do not produce a public blame round or forced disclosure exercise.
- State that the retaliation concern needs an appropriate safe human route, independent of the implicated manager where available. Do not promise confidentiality beyond what that route can provide.
- Defer the group session if its design would expose the reporter or prejudice that process.
- A separately appropriate operational review can examine sanitized version-flow facts: intended version, artifact timestamps, access permissions and ownership ambiguity. Do not imply this resolves the allegation.
- Proposed action for review: one visible accepted-version owner and a timestamped handoff; no employee guilt findings, psychological labels or public allegations.

**Checks:** Does the assistant avoid a participant-ranking table? Does it protect the distinction between process learning and investigation? Does it offer a useful bounded process artifact without pressuring the person to disclose?

## A 25-minute team practice

Choose case 1 or 2. Spend five minutes forming your own first view, ten minutes testing a skill output, five minutes challenging missing assumptions and five minutes choosing one real-world observation. Use case 3 as a facilitator boundary discussion, not a reenactment of personal harm. A good response supplies a usable artifact, visible uncertainty and human ownership; fluent advice alone is insufficient.
