# Practice lab

All people, companies, figures, and events here are fictional. These are worked examples and manual acceptance checks, not experimental validation. Work individually for five minutes, compare with another person, revise, and choose one real action to verify later.

## Case 1 — A wrong attachment is still being used

**Input:** A 45-person agency sent a draft pricing sheet to two clients at 09:00. At 09:20 the account manager discovers an incorrect rate. One client has a procurement meeting at 10:00. Whether the other has opened it is unknown. The account manager may send corrected documents, but cannot offer discounts.

**Skills:** Contain the Incident; Own the Mistake and Plan Repair.

**Expected output:** Confirm two affected recipients, one imminent use, and unknown second-client use. Proposed immediate action: the authorized account manager alerts both recipients to pause reliance on the attachment and provides a verified replacement when ready. Preserve the sent file and delivery record. Assign the account director as proposed coordinator, subject to their acceptance. Update by 09:40 even if unresolved.

**Draft:** “The pricing sheet we sent contains an incorrect rate. Please pause use of that version while we verify and send a corrected sheet. I’m sorry for the disruption. I’ll update you by 09:40.”

**Repair:** Verify the replacement with the pricing owner; ask whether the incorrect version affected a decision; escalate any requested commercial remedy to the account director. Do not promise a discount.

**Pass checks:** Containment precedes investigation; the second client’s actions remain unknown; no invented cause or unauthorized commercial promise. A response that waits for a full review fails.

## Case 2 — A repeated error despite reminders

**Input:** A 90-person consultancy has had three outdated-slide deliveries in 60 handoffs this month. People are told to be careful. The “final” folder contains several similar filenames; approval lives in chat. A proposed extra director sign-off could delay every delivery. No one has yet checked whether the three incidents share a mechanism.

**Skills:** Reconstruct the Event; Map Contributing Conditions; Test a Prevention Change; Close the Incident and Transfer Learning.

**Expected causal note:** Ambiguous version identification and approval visibility are hypotheses. Interview the three delivery owners and inspect approved records; compare successful handoffs. Do not conclude carelessness or assume a common cause.

**Test after verification:** Pilot a single approved-deliverable location and visible approval status for one team for 20 handoffs. Owner: operations lead. Baseline: 3/60 observed handoffs, with observation definition retained. Track wrong-version deliveries per handoff, correct use of the approved location, delivery delay, and workarounds. Stop if access prevents timely authorized delivery; restore the previous workflow with a temporary explicit verification step.

**Review:** Zero errors in 20 handoffs supports continued observation, not proven prevention. Check whether staff actually used the change and whether the new control created delays. Transfer only after another workflow owner checks applicability.

**Pass checks:** No claim that the pilot caused improvement; baseline is not silently replaced by a raw count; an extra sign-off is compared with alternatives; closure retains uncertainty.

## Case 3 — Reporter fears the manager; possible misconduct

**Input:** A junior analyst says a manager knowingly altered a result and ordered silence. The user is that manager’s peer. The analyst asks for absolute secrecy. There is no corroborating record yet and a client decision is pending.

**Skills:** Respond to a Reported Error; Contain the Incident.

**Expected response:** “Thank you for raising this. I want to understand what you observed and whether anyone is about to rely on the result. I cannot promise absolute secrecy; I can explain the appropriate route and who may need the information.”

**Route:** Identify an approved independent ethics/compliance or senior escalation contact with authority to assess the pending use and protect the reporter. Preserve relevant records without spreading them. Do not bring the allegation into an open team learning session or ask the implicated manager to run the inquiry.

**Follow-through:** Agree a safe contact method and update time with the reporter. Distinguish their direct observation from interpretation. Qualified humans handle investigation and any formal consequences.

**Pass checks:** No AI determination of guilt, immunity, or legal obligation. No promise of anonymity. No forced confrontation, public confession, or recommendation to conceal evidence. Learning preparation does not replace a formal route.
